Classic Review

blink-182

blink-182

Geffen Records · November 2003

9.0 Our score Essential Album

After years of jokes and fast, bratty singles, blink-182 went quiet and moody on their self-titled fifth album. The band rented a house in San Diego, filled it with vintage gear, and spent most of 2003 with producer Jerry Finn experimenting with sounds they'd never tried before.

The result is darker and more mature than anything they'd made. "Feeling This" still has the old energy, but "I Miss You" swaps power chords for brushed drums and an upright bass, and "All of This" brings in Robert Smith of The Cure.

The sound of a pop-punk band growing up in public, and getting it right.

It turned out to be their last album with Finn and their last new music before a long hiatus, which only makes it feel more like a turning point. Fans who grew up with the band's early records are still arguing about it, which is usually a sign of a great one.

Twenty-plus years later, songs like "Down" and "Always" sound less like a phase and more like the moment a band found a second identity.

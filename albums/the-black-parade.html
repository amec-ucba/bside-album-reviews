20th Anniversary Review

The Black Parade

My Chemical Romance

Reprise Records · October 2006

9.6 Our score Essential Album

Twenty years on, The Black Parade still sounds like a band betting everything on one idea. My Chemical Romance built a full concept album around a character called the Patient, a man dying young, and imagined death arriving the way he remembered his happiest childhood day: as a marching band.

The band went all the way with it. They recorded with producer Rob Cavallo, dressed in black marching-band uniforms on stage, and played as their own alter ego, the Black Parade. What could have been a gimmick became a statement about grief, regret and staying alive, wrapped in some of the biggest choruses of the decade.

It's a funeral, a rock opera and a pep rally, often in the same song.

"Welcome to the Black Parade" is the centerpiece: it starts as a single piano note and a memory, then marches its way into a full-band anthem. Around it, the album swings from the theatrical stomp of "Mama," which features Liza Minnelli, to the stripped-down "I Don't Love You" and the sneering "Teenagers."

Plenty of 2000s rock records were loud. Few were this ambitious, or this sure of what they wanted to say. Two decades later, it holds up as the high point of an era, and as a reminder that big, sincere and a little ridiculous can be the same thing.
